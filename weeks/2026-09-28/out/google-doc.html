MON 9/28 · THE OPEN · Win the First 10 Seconds

9:00 CROWN CALL: Goal board. Every agent stands up and says his week out loud: IP goal, sits goal, and one thing he's fixing from last week. Leader writes them on the board, and they stay up until Saturday's EOW.

9:03 THE LESSON: The client decides whether to stay on the line before you finish your second sentence. Sound like you're following up on their request, not selling them something.

  • Say why you're calling in the first sentence. In Gong's cold-call research, openers that stated a clear reason for the call did about 2× better.
  • Never open with "Is this a bad time?" Gong found that line made a booked next step about 40% less likely. It hands them the exit.
  • Drop your tone at the end of each sentence and slow down 10%. Rising tone sounds like asking permission. Flat, calm tone sounds like a professional following up.
  • Speed to lead: call new leads within 5 minutes. The MIT / InsideSales study found contact was 100× more likely at 5 minutes than at 30.

Steal this line: “Hey John, it's Mike. I'm getting back to you on the life insurance request you sent in. I've got it pulled up right here. Was that just for you, or for your wife too?”

9:13 LIVE REPS · Speed Dating: Two lines facing each other. The agent delivers his opener while the client flips a reaction card: hangs up, "Who is this?", "I didn't fill anything out", friendly, or suspicious. 90 seconds, then the line shifts one seat. Six rounds, so everyone opens six times before 9:30.

9:27 LOCK-IN: First sit booked by 10:30. Before your first dial, write your opener on a sticky note on your monitor, reason for the call included.

TUE 9/29 · THE CONVERSATION · Ask, Don't Tell

9:00 CROWN CALL: Yesterday's scoreboard: top dialer, most sits, every sale. Each sale gets a bell ring and a 10-second breakdown from the agent of what worked.

9:03 THE LESSON: People buy for their reasons, not yours. Jeremy Miner's NEPQ method is built on this: questions let the client talk himself into the problem, so you never have to push.

  • The NEPQ path: Connect → Situation → Problem → Solution → Consequence → Commit. Don't skip to the product.
  • Situation: "Who's the policy for? Anything in place right now?"
  • Problem: "If something happened tomorrow, who'd be left handling everything? How would they pay for it?"
  • Consequence: "What would that mean for her?" Then stop talking. Silence does the work.
  • Tone is curious and a little slow, as if you genuinely want to know. You do.

Steal this line: “What made you want to look into this now, instead of putting it off another year?”

9:13 LIVE REPS · Question Ladder: Pairs. The agent has to ask 5 questions in a row with no pitching and no statements. If he makes a statement, his partner yells "BUZZ" and he starts over at question 1. Three minutes a side, then swap and run it again with a harder client.

9:27 LOCK-IN: Write your 3 best "why now" questions on an index card and tape it next to your phone. Use all three on your first sit.

WED 9/30 · CEO WEDNESDAY · Protect the Bag: Client Retention

9:00 CROWN CALL: Chargeback check. Leader reads one real (anonymous) chargeback from the book and asks the room: "What would have saved this one?"

9:03 THE LESSON: A sale isn't a sale until it has been on the books for 12 months. A $60 policy that sticks beats a $120 policy that lapses, because the lapse comes back out of your pocket.

  • Match the draft date to their deposit. Social Security pays on the 2nd, 3rd or 4th Wednesday depending on birthday (1st–10th, 11th–20th, 21st–31st). SSI pays on the 1st. People who started benefits before May 1997 are paid on the 3rd.
  • Read the bank and routing numbers back out loud and confirm the draft date twice before you hang up.
  • Send a welcome text within 24 hours: what to expect, that the carrier may call, and a letter in the mail.
  • Check in 2 days before the first draft. The Discord bot reminds you, so don't ignore it.
  • Don't stretch the budget. Sell the premium they can pay every month, not the biggest one they'll say yes to today.

Steal this line: “I want this to be there when your family actually needs it, so let's line the draft up with when your check hits. What day does your Social Security come in?”

9:13 LIVE REPS · Close the Loop: Pairs run only the last 3 minutes of the call: draft date, bank read-back, what to expect, referral ask with the reward card. The leader grades on a 5-box checklist. Five of five or you run it again.

9:27 LOCK-IN: Before your first dial, open Command, find every draft date hitting in the next 7 days, and send each of those clients a check-in text.

THU 10/1 · BATTLE FOR THE CROWN · Objection Battle: Brush-Offs & Stalls

9:00 CROWN CALL: Walkouts. Each contestant (every agent in his first 90 days, plus any vet who wants smoke) walks out to his song. The room gets loud. Throw-Down Thursday starts here.

9:03 THE LESSON: Two-minute refresher before the fight: acknowledge, isolate, then ask a question that moves forward. Most early objections are reflexes, not decisions.

  • Acknowledge: "Totally fair." No arguing and no defending.
  • Isolate: "Other than that, is there anything else that would stop you?"
  • Ask forward: always finish on a question that moves toward the next step.
  • Hormozi's lens: every objection is about circumstances (time, money), other people (spouse, kids), or self ("I'm not sure it's for me"). Name which one it is before you answer.

Steal this line: “Totally fair. Most people say that until they see the numbers. Can I ask you two quick questions, and if it's not a fit, I'll tell you myself?”

9:13 LIVE REPS · Battle for the Crown: Full rules on the Battle page. Each round the leader plays the client and hits every contestant with the same objection. After each round the room votes, and the lowest vote is out. The last man standing takes the Crown and the prize.

9:27 LOCK-IN: The winner gives a 30-second breakdown of his best handle. Everyone else writes down the one line they're stealing from him.

FRI 10/2 · FILM ROOM · Tape Doesn't Lie: Tonality

9:00 CROWN CALL: Bounty reveal. The leader posts today's bounty (for example, first sit before 10 AM gets lunch paid) and plays the one best 20-second clip of the week.

9:03 THE LESSON: We break down tape like a football team. Jordan Belfort's rule is that the client needs to feel certain about three things: the product, you, and the company. Tonality is where certainty shows up or dies.

  • Tape 1 is the best call of the week. Pause on the exact moment the client leaned in and ask the room what the agent did.
  • Tape 2 is a lost call. Pause on the moment it died, and the room calls out the better line.
  • Listen for certainty drops: uptalk, filler words, rushing the price, apologizing.
  • Compliance: only play calls recorded with proper consent. Florida requires every party to consent to a recording.

Steal this line: “Based on everything you told me, this is exactly what I'd do if it were my own mom.”

9:13 LIVE REPS · Say It 5 Ways: Take the line where the lost call died. Five agents deliver it in five tones: curious, certain, caring, urgent, and "reasonable man." The room votes on the winner, and that version goes into the script notes.

9:27 LOCK-IN: Name one line you're cutting and one you're adding. Post both in #wins before 9:45.

BATTLE FOR THE CROWN

Objection elimination. Last man standing wears the Crown.

  1. Contestants: every agent in his first 90 days is in. Vets can volunteer to defend.
  2. The leader plays the client. Every contestant gets the same objection each round and has 45–60 seconds to handle it.
  3. After each round the room votes. Raise a hand for each contestant, or use a Discord poll for remote agents. The lowest vote is out. On a tie, the tied contestants go to sudden death with a new objection.
  4. Vote on four things: CONTROL (did he keep the frame), TONE (calm and certain), QUESTION (did he end with a question that moves forward) and CLEAN (no false promises, nothing he couldn't say to compliance).
  5. The final two face the Boss Round: three objections stacked back to back without a break.
  6. The winner holds the physical Crown at his desk all week, picks the walkout song for next battle, and takes the prize.

ROUND 1 · BRUSH-OFFS: "I'm not interested." / "How did you get my number?" / "I didn't fill anything out."
Strong handle: Acknowledge, then remind them of the request with specifics (date, what they asked about) and ask a light question. "No worries. It came in on Tuesday asking about coverage for final expenses. Was that for you or someone else?"

ROUND 2 · STALLS: "Just mail me something." / "Call me back next week."
Strong handle: The mail is where deals go to die. "I can, but most of it won't make sense without your age and health. Can I ask two quick questions so what I send is actually your price?"

ROUND 3 · THE THINKER: "I need to think about it."
Strong handle: Isolate. "Totally. Usually when someone says that, it's the price, the coverage, or whether they trust it. Which one is it for you?"

BOSS ROUND · STACKED: "I need to talk to my wife" → "Money's tight right now" → "I don't give my bank info over the phone."
Strong handle: One objection at a time, calm tone, a question at the end of each. Winners slow down when the pressure goes up.

Prize (leadership sets it): e.g., $100 in lead credit plus lunch on the team

Culture Events

  • THE CROWN: A physical crown (or belt) that moves to every Battle winner. It sits on his desk all week.
  • THE BELL: Every sale rings the bell in the office and gets a post in #bell on Discord. No sale goes unheard.
  • WALKOUT SONGS: Every agent picks one. Battle contestants walk out to theirs.
  • BOUNTY BOARD: Daily bounties from leadership: first sit before 10, most dials by lunch, first IUL of the week.
  • DRAFT DAY: Captains snake-draft teams for the next week's Throw-Down competition.
  • SHARK TANK: 60-second recruiting pitches scored by a 3-person panel. It builds the agency-building muscle.
  • REVERSE ROLE PLAY: Leaders play the agent and agents play the nightmare client. The room learns by watching the pros get tested.

Research Digest

  • Jeremy Miner (NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning)): Ask questions that let the client talk himself into the problem: Connect → Situation → Problem → Solution → Consequence → Commit. Curious tone, no pushing.
  • Jordan Belfort (Straight Line System): The client must rate the product, you, and the company a 10 out of 10 on certainty. Tonality carries certainty. Loop objections back to certainty instead of arguing.
  • Chris Voss (Tactical empathy (Never Split the Difference)): Labels ("It sounds like…"), mirrors (repeat their last 1–3 words), an accusation audit that names their fears first, and "how/what" questions. Go for "That's right" instead of "You're right."
  • Alex Hormozi (CLOSER framework · Rule of 100): Clarify, Label, Overview past pain, Sell the vacation, Explain away concerns, Reinforce. Every objection is circumstances, other people, or self. Rule of 100: 100 primary actions a day for 100 days.
  • Jeb Blount (Fanatical Prospecting · Objections): The 30-Day Rule: today's prospecting pays off over the next 90 days. Protect your Golden Hours for selling only. For brush-offs, use Ledge → Disrupt → Ask.
  • Andy Elliott (Daily reps and high-standard sales culture): Role play every single day, loud and in front of the room. Standards and energy are trained like skills.
  • Grant Cardone (The 10X Rule): Set targets 10× bigger and match them with massive action. Most people fail from too little activity, not too much.
  • David Duford (Final-expense telesales training): Persistency is the business: match draft dates to deposit days, sell a premium they can keep, and follow up before the first draft.
  • Cody Askins (8% Nation (insurance agents)): Follow-up and personal brand compound. Post daily, show receipts, and treat your book of business like a company.
  • 100×: Contact is 100× more likely when you call within 5 minutes instead of 30 (MIT / InsideSales Lead Response Management study).
  • 2×: Openers that state a reason for the call perform about 2× better. "Is this a bad time?" cuts results by roughly 40% (Gong Labs cold-call research).
  • 1 in 7: LIMRA has long reported that only about 1 in 7 new agents is still in the business four years later. Culture and training are the retention plan.
  • 12 MO: The chargeback window. A lapse inside it comes out of your pocket and can cost you carrier contracts.

Stay Clean

  • Calling hours: federal law allows 8 AM–9 PM in the client's local time. Florida is stricter at 8 AM–8 PM, and it limits repeat solicitation calls to the same person within 24 hours. Check each state with your IMO or compliance before dialing late.
  • Recording: Florida requires every party's consent to record a call. Only use disclosed and consented recordings in the Film Room.
  • No rebating: the reward card goes out whether the client buys or not. Sale posts show the client's first name and last initial only.
